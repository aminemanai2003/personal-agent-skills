# Profile And Proof Audit

## First-Scan Test

A reviewer should be able to answer these questions quickly:

1. What role or problem space is this person targeting?
2. What are the two or three strongest relevant capabilities?
3. What evidence supports those capabilities?
4. What did this person personally own or contribute?
5. Where can the work be inspected?

If the answers require reading every section, improve hierarchy before adding more content.

## Section Guidance

### Headline

Use a compact combination of role direction, capability, and domain or evidence. Keep official current status accurate. A student or early-career candidate can state the target honestly without presenting it as an existing senior title.

### About

- Open with the current technical or professional direction.
- Support it with one or two specific evidence statements.
- Mention representative work and the difficult part handled.
- Add domain interest or collaboration style only when it differentiates the candidate.
- Close with a concrete, non-desperate signal about relevant opportunities or collaboration when useful.

### Experience And Projects

Distinguish:

- personal ownership from team outcomes;
- shipped or deployed work from prototypes;
- measured results from observations;
- client or employer work from independent projects;
- current capabilities from skills being learned.

### Featured And Portfolio Proof

For every featured artifact, provide:

- problem and intended user;
- constraints and meaningful technical decisions;
- candidate's exact contribution;
- link to code, demo, paper, screenshots, or write-up;
- result and current status;
- limitation or next step when material.

## Consistency Check

Compare names, titles, dates, technologies, outcomes, and links across resume, LinkedIn, GitHub, portfolio, and application materials. Resolve real conflicts; do not mechanically force every surface to use identical wording.
