# Natural Expert Prose

## Positive standard

- Write from understood material and a real communicative purpose.
- Let the argument determine paragraph length, section order, and sentence rhythm.
- Use precise domain terms where they reduce ambiguity.
- Connect claims through actual causality, contrast, evidence, or consequence.
- Keep a visible point of view while distinguishing judgment from fact.
- Revise at paragraph and sentence level after the factual structure is stable.

## Common synthetic patterns to remove

- An opening that announces importance without naming a concrete problem.
- Repeated three-item lists chosen for rhythm rather than completeness.
- Headings that divide material into mechanically equal sections.
- Transitions such as "it is important to note" that add no reasoning.
- Repetition of the same claim in the introduction, each section ending, and conclusion.
- Excessive abstract nouns where a person, action, mechanism, or result can be named.
- Unsupported adjectives such as robust, seamless, transformative, comprehensive, or groundbreaking.
- Generic conclusions that recommend more research without identifying the unresolved question.

Do not replace these patterns mechanically. Reconstruct the passage from its evidence, audience, and intended effect.
